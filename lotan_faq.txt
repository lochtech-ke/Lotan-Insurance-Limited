Q: What is a Loan Enablement Instrument (Credit Protection Policy)?
A: A Loan Enablement Instrument is a structured risk-transfer tool that converts a lender’s exposure to borrower default into an insurable credit event. It helps unlock lending capacity and reduces capital costs by improving lender confidence. From a financial engineering perspective, it functions as a contingent protection wrapper around a debt exposure.

Q: What are the benefits for Lenders using the Credit Protection Policy?
A: For lenders, the policy:
- Reduces risk-weighted asset (RWA) pressure.
- Supports Basel III and Basel IV capital optimization.
- Improves return on regulatory capital by lowering capital consumption.
- Enables higher approval rates for non-investment-grade or thin-file obligors.
- Supports tighter pricing, longer tenor, and improved advance rates.

Q: What are the benefits for Borrowers using the Credit Protection Policy?
A: For borrowers, the policy:
- Creates access to credit where the balance sheet alone might be insufficient.
- Improves pricing, tenor, and facility size by strengthening lender confidence.
- Protects operating capacity during temporary distress.
- Reduces reliance on personal guarantees by substituting a structured mechanism.
- Improves credibility with international lenders and export financiers.

Q: What are your project security instruments?
A: We offer several instruments designed for project financing and mobilization:
1. Performance Bonds: Typically 2%-5% of contract value, guaranteeing contractor performance and project completion.
2. Advance Payment Guarantees: Protects the owner’s mobilization payment until applied to the works, preserving contractor liquidity.
3. Bid Bonds (Competitive Financing Tool): Ensures that a contractor will enter into a contract at the bid price if selected, demonstrating financial capability without tying up excessive cash.

Q: What is Mobilization Capital?
A: Mobilization Capital refers to our solutions that unlock working capital for project startup by securing advance payments and early procurement commitments. This helps preserve liquidity before progress billings begin.
